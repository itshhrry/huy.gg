biology, then operations, then data

I spent a decade working inside healthcare operations, and that experience shaped how I think, work, and approach data. Healthcare taught me that a row in a table is somebody's appointment, somebody's bill, or somebody's discharge. That awareness does not leave you, and I think it makes for better analysis.

It started with science

My academic foundation began in biology and chemistry. Those disciplines taught me to think rigorously, question assumptions, and follow the evidence wherever it leads. That mindset never left. It found a new home in data.

A decade in healthcare operations

After the M.S. at Denver I worked across frontline operations, regional management, and revenue cycle, including work with the State of Colorado where strict regulatory environments became routine.

I understood how organisations run, where the inefficiencies hide, and what questions leadership actually needs answered. The information was always there. What was missing was someone to turn it into something actionable.

the project that set the direction

During my master's programme I worked with Centura Health on a capstone evaluating and simplifying clinical workflows under High-Reliability Organization principles. We built a system that processed real-time data from Epic, patient monitoring devices, and administrative databases, then applied predictive analytics and machine learning to forecast admissions, resource utilisation, and potential complications.

The outcomes fed the ulcerative colitis protocol, resource allocation during COVID-19, and a reduction in operational cost. That project set the direction for everything after it.

mit capstone  ·  facial emotion recognition, final result
Four model iterations. A baseline artificial neural network reached 56 percent F1, a simple convolutional network without dropout 59 percent, a refined one 69 percent, and the optimised architecture 91 percent across happy, sad, surprised, and neutral. Depth, dropout tuning, and epoch scaling did the work; matching grayscale input to what the model expected removed preprocessing artefacts.
