# Overall Impression

The writing on this site is better than the design around it, and the design is better than the shipped defaults that decide what a visitor actually sees. Nothing here is broken in a way a visitor would report. Almost everything is degraded in a way a visitor would feel and not name.

The single biggest opportunity is that **the theme a recruiter lands on was never designed.** All five pages ship `data-theme="light"` on the root and have since the redesign deployed. The light tokens were not composed against a light ground; they were produced by pulling contrast out of the dark ones. `--flare` collapses to the same value as `--accent`, so the two-tone accent system flattens to one tone. The aurora glows drop from `.32` and `.5` to `.16` and `.12`, so the atmosphere that gives the dark hero its point of view becomes an invisible haze. The grain inverts to `multiply` at `.5` and reads as grime. And `body{background:#08090B}` is still hard-coded on every page, so near-black sits behind the cream ground and shows as a flash on load and as black gutters under rubber-band scroll.

That is not a polish problem. The primary surface is a de-tuned copy of the secondary one.

# What's Working

**The three-variant resume is a real strategic idea, executed.** `resume-data.js` tags every individual bullet with the variants it belongs to (`tabs: ['analyst','revenue']`), so switching to Analyst recomposes the document from the bullet up rather than hiding blocks. Paired with a framing note that changes per tab, it answers the reader's actual question, which is whether this candidate fits *their* opening. This is the one thing on the site a template could not produce.

**The case-study prose does what the design principles promise.** The Carbon section explains a decision and its consequence in one breath instead of listing a stack, pairs it with a caption saying what the screenshot actually is, and writes alt text to the same standard. Claims land as floors rather than counts, so "Over 2,300 automated tests" survives the next commit.

**The responsive foundation is measurably sound, and neither agent expected it.** Zero horizontal overflow and zero heading overflow at 375px, 768px, and 1280px, on all four pages, measured rather than eyeballed. There is not a single `@media` query anywhere in the site; responsiveness comes entirely from 117 `clamp()` calls and 9 `minmax()` declarations. Console is clean on all four pages: zero errors, zero warnings, every request 200, no third-party runtime requests. Every `<img>` on the site has descriptive, non-empty alt text.

# Priority Issues

## [P1] The default theme fails AA in 21 places on the homepage alone

**What.** `--muted2` at `#837C70` fails 4.5:1 against **every ground in the light theme**, measured at 3.18 to 4.13 across `--bg`, both panel-gradient endpoints, `--card-rim`, and both surface tints. The same token passes everywhere in dark, at 5.41 to 6.26. Measured failures per page, light theme: index 21 elements (12 unique combinations), resume 10, projects 5, about 1. Dark theme: index 0, about 0, resume 0, projects 1.

The worst are not marginal. "Retention" and "NPS" inside the hero card measure **2.88:1** at 11px. "Professional Certificate" measures **2.98:1**. "Advanced Urgent Care, 14 clinics, Regional Ops" measures 3.18:1 at 10.5px. The copyright line, every project card's tech line, and every faux-window chrome label sit at 3.66:1. Separately, `--accent` at `#C04000` fails on the card rim and both surface tints (4.06 to 4.37), which catches "View Resume", "Regional Ops", and both university names.

Both assessments reached these numbers independently. The design review computed 3.65:1 and 4.69:1 by hand from the token literals; the measurement pass returned 3.66 and 4.68 from live computed styles with transitions disabled. The arithmetic and the instrument agree to two decimal places.

**Why it matters.** PRODUCT.md sets WCAG 2.1 AA as the bar and predicted this exact failure. The prediction was correct and the re-verification had not been done. A recruiter reading on a phone in an office loses the smallest, most factual text on the page: clinic counts, credential lines, and tech stacks. This is a site arguing for precision, failing a measurable standard, in the theme it chose as its front door.

**Fix.** Darken `--muted2` to at least `#6B6459` for a 4.6:1 floor on `--bg`, and check it again against `#DDD7CC` and `#E8D8CC`, which are the two grounds where it currently reads 2.88 and 2.98. Give `--flare` a distinct light-mode value so the two-tone system survives. Stop using `--accent` on tinted surfaces at 12px, or darken it there. Delete `background:#08090B` from `body` on all five pages and move it behind a `[data-theme="dark"]` selector.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable audit` then `$impeccable colorize`

## [P1] The site has no identity in a browser tab or a pasted link

**What.** `grep -c "<title"` returns 0 on every page. `document.title` returns empty. There is no `<meta name="description">`, no Open Graph tags, no Twitter Card, and no favicon `<link>` despite `favicon.ico` sitting in the repo at 15,867 bytes. Both `<head>`s carry live `preconnect` hints to `fonts.googleapis.com` and `fonts.gstatic.com` for a webfont that no longer exists, opening two DNS and TLS handshakes to Google on every page load for zero benefit.

**Why it matters.** PRODUCT.md describes the audience as arriving "with a stack of other candidates open in adjacent tabs." That is literally how screeners work: middle-click every link in an application into background tabs, then work through them. An untitled tab is an unidentifiable candidate in that stack. And when a screener passes a candidate upward, they paste the URL into Slack, Teams, or an ATS note, where it unfurls as a bare domain with no name, no role, and no summary. The site loses its identity at the exact moment it is being recommended.

**Fix.** Per-page `<title>` leading with the name and role frame. A one-sentence `meta description` per page drawn from existing hero copy. `og:title`, `og:description`, and `og:image` using a purpose-built card rather than the logo. A favicon link to the file already sitting there. Delete both dead preconnects. Add `<meta name="color-scheme">` while in the head, since its absence makes the browser paint the wrong scrollbar and form-control colors after the theme toggle.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`

## [P1] "NPS 92%" is a domain error in 52px type

**What.** Net Promoter Score is an index running −100 to +100. It is not a percentage. The source in `resume-data.js` reads "89 to 93% net promoter scores", already wrong, and the homepage hardens the middle of that range into a single number with a percent sign. It sits inside the hero card, which is the largest object on the page. "Retention 100%" beside it carries no timeframe and no denominator.

**Why it matters.** The audience screens analysts. A mis-stated metric is the specific error they are trained to catch, and the number a reader can check is the one that is wrong, which invites them to discount the numbers they cannot check. Both halves of the first impression become "template" and "loose with numbers", which are the two things this site exists to disprove.

**Fix.** Write it as a score: "89 to 93 net promoter score." Give retention a period and a population. If the number stays in the hero at all, put it in prose where it can carry its context rather than in a stat cell where it cannot.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

## [P1] The primary evidence path drops visitors 6,800 pixels from what they clicked

**What.** `index.html:180` and the two project cards at lines 233 and 253 link into `projects.html#rcm`, `#emotion`, and `#infra`. Measured live: navigating to `projects.html#infra` lands at `scrollY = 63` while `#infra` sits at `y = 6835`. `scroll-margin-top: 96px` is correctly declared, so the CSS is not the problem. `#rcm` at y=3676 works; `#infra` at y=6835 does not.

The cause is load order. **No `<img>` on any page carries `width` or `height` attributes**, zero of twelve, so the document is short when the browser performs the fragment scroll and grows past the target as images decode. projects.html ships **2.22MB of images with no `loading="lazy"` on any of them**: `rcm-report-analysis.png` is 943KB to fill a 283×132 box, and `rcm-report-summary.png` is 757KB for a 283×123 box. That is 1.70MB for two thumbnails, a linear over-delivery of roughly 9.6 times. The deeper the anchor, the more reliably it fails, and the two that fail are the two the homepage cards point at.

A separate 128KB is wasted on every page load site-wide: `logo-cream.png` is the dark-theme logo, hidden by `[data-theme="light"] [data-logo-dark]{display:none}`. The default theme is light, so it is fetched over the network and rendered at 0×0 on all four pages. Confirmed in the network log as a 200 on every navigation.

**Why it matters.** A recruiter clicks the specific project they were interested in and is deposited, without explanation, at the top of the longest page on the site. On a phone this is the default outcome rather than an edge case, because that is where the payload hurts most.

**Fix.** Add explicit `width` and `height` to all twelve images so layout reserves space before decode. Add `loading="lazy"` below the first screen. Re-apply `location.hash` on `window.load` as a correction. Resize the two RCM PNGs to something proportionate to a 283px box, or crop them to a legible fragment instead of shrinking the whole report. Load `logo-cream.png` conditionally, or serve one logo and recolor it in CSS.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable optimize`

## [P2] `prefers-reduced-motion` does not exist anywhere in the codebase

**What.** `grep -c "prefers-reduced-motion"` returns 0 on all four pages. There are zero `@media` blocks of any kind. The suppression mechanism exists as `[data-rm="1"] *{animation-duration:.001s!important;...}`, but `data-rm` is only ever set by a hard-coded `false` in the boot call at `index.html:460`, and `#home-root` ships `data-rm="0"`. The override cannot fire without a manual in-page action.

A visitor with the OS setting enabled therefore receives: two aurora blobs at 16s and 20s, a 26s marquee, a 6s infinite gradient shine on the headline, a 7s floaty bob on the hero card, a 30s looping canvas bird flying across the h1, staggered section reveals, and count-up animations. `html{scroll-behavior:smooth}` and the root's `transition: background .4s` are unguarded even by the manual override. The `phoenix()` render loop calls `requestAnimationFrame` unconditionally and forever, so it keeps burning frames long after the cycle has nothing left to draw.

**Why it matters.** Beyond the accessibility failure, this is the clearest case on the site of a claim not matching the artifact. I wrote into PRODUCT.md that reduced motion was honored, having read the mechanism without checking what drives it. That line has been corrected. On a site whose first design principle is that claims pair with something checkable, the product document itself failed the principle.

**Fix.** Move it to CSS so it works before scripts run and cannot be bypassed: `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` setting the suppression rules directly. Suppress the bird and the aurora entirely rather than shortening them to 1ms, since a 1ms animation still teleports. Guard `scroll-behavior`. Terminate the `phoenix()` rAF loop when the cycle ends and when `document.hidden`.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable animate`

# Minor Observations

- **Two genuine tab-order divergences on index.html**, both confirmed by a live 20-keystroke `focusin` log rather than computed alone. The hero card link at y=277 is 6th visually and 8th in tab order, so focus jumps down to the CTAs at y=672 and then back up 400px. The featured-project image link at y=1384 is 9th visually and 11th in tab order, jumping back up 360px. The other three pages match visual order.
- **Focus is visible but unstyled.** Zero `:focus` or `:focus-visible` rules exist across all four files, and there is exactly one `outline` declaration on the entire site, `outline:none` at `projects.html:404`. All 19 focusable elements on index report the Chrome default ring, `outline: auto 1px rgb(229,151,0)`, untuned against either ground. PRODUCT.md asks for a designed focus state; this is the inherited one.
- **No skip link on any page.** Every page starts the tab sequence with five header controls before any content.
- **The resume tabs have no ARIA at all.** Three bare `<button data-tab>` elements: no `role="tablist"`, no `aria-selected`, no `aria-controls`, no `role="tabpanel"`, no `aria-live`, no arrow-key navigation. The content swaps silently for assistive tech. This is a WCAG 4.1.2 name-role-value failure on the best feature the site has.
- **No `prefers-color-scheme` and no `matchMedia` anywhere.** `data-theme="light"` is hard-coded, so a visitor whose OS is set to dark receives the light theme, which is the theme carrying all the contrast failures.
- **The theme toggle has no `aria-pressed`** and a static label, so a screen-reader user gets no state; only the icon changes, and it is decorative SVG with no accessible name.
- **The copy breaks the project's own writing rules** in at least six places: "leveraging data" and "passionate about this work" (about, section 05), "streamline clinical workflows", "helped streamline ulcerative colitis protocol", and "optimize resource allocation" (section 03), and "Ensured scheduling efficiency" in `resume-data.js`. Also "reduce significant operational costs", unquantified.
- **about.html prose runs 78 to 93 characters per line** at 1188px against a 65 to 75ch cap, on the page where the writing is the strongest asset, and leaves the right 40% of the viewport empty for the page's full height.
- **A duplicated sentence reads as an editing miss.** `about.html:103` ends "The information was always there." and `:105` opens the pull quote "The information was there. What was missing was..."
- **The largest evidence artifact on the homepage is illegible.** `rcm-report-summary.png` is 757KB rendered at 532×230, a dense financial report reduced to a gray smear of digits. It conveys that numbers exist and nothing more.
- **The `--strong` token is `#000000` in light and `#ffffff` in dark**, neither tinted toward its ground, so bolded inline text sits slightly outside the palette.
- **The marquee lists eleven technologies with no framing**, which is exactly the "listing the technologies involved" the brand personality says the voice does not do.
- **Zero imagery of the person**, on a site whose stated personality is warm, human, and approachable, including an about page whose h1 is "Behind every data point is a person."

# Questions to Consider

1. If the dark theme is where the design has a point of view and the light theme is that same layout with the contrast removed, is the honest fix to design a light theme, or to admit the dark one is the brand and solve "warm and human" with a face, a voice, and a photograph rather than a background color?
2. `#F4F1EA` is the single most-generated background color of 2026. What would warmth look like if it came from a saturated ground the brand owns, a terracotta or a deep clay, with the type doing the calming instead?
3. Every glyph on this site is Arial, and 103 inline declarations mark where a second family used to live. What is the physical object this brand is? A clinic intake form. A payer remittance advice. A hospital wayfinding sign. Pick one, find its typeface, and the label system that currently reads flat gets its voice back.
4. The three-variant resume is the only idea here a template could not produce. What if it were the homepage, and the first screen asked which role you are hiring for?
5. Six CTAs compete above the fold and the one conversion that matters, email, is ten screens down on a phone with nothing in the nav. What happens to the ninety seconds if there is exactly one action on the first screen?
6. The most human sentence on four pages is about ube desserts, parked in a floating card on the page fewest people open. If warm and human is the brand, why is the warmth rationed to one card?
7. There is an eight-month gap on the most recent role and the site says nothing about it. What would a confident version of this page say, out loud, in one line, right where the eye lands?
